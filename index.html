World- and Action-supervised Latent Actions

WALA

Learning Executable Latent Actions from Action-Labeled Demonstrations and Action-Free Videos

CASIA & Anyverse Dynamics Team

arXiv Paper GitHub Code

Abstract

Human videos capture rich physical interactions at a scale difficult to reproduce with robots, yet many cannot directly supervise robot policies because action annotations are absent or incompatible with robot action spaces. Latent action models can learn action-related representations from observation sequences without action annotations, providing a route for such videos to contribute to robot learning.

We present WALA, a policy-learning framework that couples both the encoder and decoder of a pretrained LAM to the policy-generated latent action tokens that directly feed the robot action head. Its semantic-geometric LAM represents interaction changes through complementary DINOv3 feature and depth-map deltas, capturing semantic changes without reconstructing future RGB appearance while preserving fine-grained spatial geometry.

Encoder distillation and decoder prediction refine these policy-generated latent action tokens on both videos without action annotations and robot demonstrations. On robot demonstrations, action prediction additionally grounds the same tokens in executable actions. This shared latent-action pathway enables task-relevant human videos to improve the resulting robot policy.

WALA-Wan achieves 92.33% average success on RoboTwin, while WALA reaches 75.2% on RoboCasa-GR1-Tabletop, 5.0 percentage points above the strongest reported baseline. On real robots, action-free egocentric human videos raise average success from 63.3% to 74.2% with 50 robot demonstrations per task and support target-task zero-shot and few-shot transfer.

Method

WALA learns semantic and geometric changes from videos, then uses them to supervise the latent action tokens that directly feed a robot action head. Action-free videos and robot demonstrations refine the same tokens; robot demonstrations additionally ground them in executable control. WALA uses Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, while WALA-Wan uses Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with an action-query resampler. Both share the same LAM supervision.

Stage I

Semantic-geometric LAM pretraining

A current frame and four sampled future frames provide semantic and geometric changes. The encoder compresses these changes into 32 latent action tokens; the decoder predicts the future deltas from those tokens and the current observation. The LAM is pretrained on 25,224 hours of human, real-robot, and simulated video.

Stage II

Policy training with LAM supervision

The frozen LAM encoder supplies distillation targets, and the trainable decoder constrains the future changes predicted from policy tokens. Robot action labels train the action head on the same tokens. At inference, only the policy backbone and action head are needed.

Human-Video Transfer

Target-task zero-shot and few-shot transfer

Task-relevant human videos improve success in all six target-task comparisons. Each setting adds 150 action-free human videos per target task, with either zero or five robot demonstrations.

For zero-shot tasks, no target-task robot demonstrations are used; only action-free human videos are provided. Action supervision from co-trained robot tasks remains available. Zero-shot transfer may benefit from the same or similar action patterns in co-trained, action-labeled robot data. Each comparison uses 10 trials per task.
